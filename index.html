About

I've just finished an MSc in Computer Science at the University of Nottingham, a four-year course I went into knowing I liked programming and came out of with a much clearer sense of which parts I like most.

The modules I got the most out of were the ones with real depth to them — machine learning, AI methods, advanced data structures and algorithms, and operating systems and concurrency. Alongside those: software engineering, computer security, and the maths underneath all of it.

What I actually enjoy is the middle part of a problem. Working out how data should move through a system, why something is slower than it should be, or what an algorithm is really doing once you stop reading the description and start tracing it. I'd rather understand something properly than get it working and move on — a slower way to start and a faster way to finish.

Most of what I know beyond the course I picked up building things I wanted to exist. A few of those are below.

Multi-agent pursuit-evasion

Python · AI

  • Cooperating hunter agents track evasive targets by sight and sound. A trained CNN recognises audio cues, a Kalman filter predicts where the target is heading, and A* handles navigation around obstacles.
  • The interesting part was getting the three to agree — a confident prediction and a noisy measurement pull in different directions.
